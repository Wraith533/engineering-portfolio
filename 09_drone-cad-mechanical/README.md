# Drone CAD & Mechanical Design

The physical side of the drone work: airframe trays, payload enclosures, camera
mounts, and radio cases — designed, iterated, and print-ready. Both parametric
(OpenSCAD) and mesh (STL) work, with rendered previews.

## What's here

- **`cad_Drone_3D/`** — the main body of work. Payload trays across many revisions
  (v7–v16), separate and combined lids, Jetson enclosure boxes (standard and
  extended), narrow-tail and flush variants, camera-tilt trays, GPS lids, and spacers.
  The version history shows real design iteration, not a single lucky part.
- **`drone_mount/`** — parametric OpenSCAD sources (`.scad`) for the tray family
  (base, cam, 3-lid, flush, Jetson-extended, Jetson-lock variants) plus battery case
  and GPS stand — the parametric originals behind many of the STLs.
- **`radio_case/`** — a full multi-part radio enclosure: body, front/back plates,
  battery housing, electronics housing, door, with assembly and layout renders.
- **`cad_radio_mod/`** — Raspberry Pi 5 / Silvus radio bodies and lids, generated by a
  Python build script (`build_radio_body.py`) with preview renders and a section check
  — parametric CAD driven from code.

## Why it matters to an employer

- Demonstrates **mechanical/CAD skill** and, in the OpenSCAD and Python-driven parts,
  **design-as-code** — parametric models that regenerate cleanly when requirements
  change.
- Closes the loop with the electronics and software projects: I can carry a drone
  subsystem from PCB (project 08) to enclosure to flight.

## Stack

OpenSCAD (parametric), Python-driven CAD, STL/3MF, FDM 3D printing.
